# Real-time score following and live music tracking (arXiv / ISMIR / ICASSP, 2015 to Nov 2026) with a focus on repeats, skips, and premature-transition avoidance

Scope note: arXiv coverage of score following is thin before 2020; the structurally most relevant work (Arzt & Widmer "any-time" tracking 2010, Arzt & Widmer multi-reference tracking ISMIR 2015, Dixon MATCH 2005) is not on arXiv, so it is cited from JKU / ISMIR / IJCAI archives. Relevance judgments below are made for a lyric-slide follower that works from Whisper word hypotheses plus phrase-onset detection; no system found does exactly that, but the opera lyrics trackers (Brazier & Widmer 2021) and the Winterreise real-time lyrics aligner (Park et al. 2024) are the closest analogues.

## Key question 1: What are the main online score following approaches (title, authors, year, id/venue, method, metrics, latency)?

### Takeaway
Two algorithm families dominate: online DTW (Dixon 2005 "OLTW", extended by Arzt/Widmer and now packaged in the open-source Matchmaker library) and HMM / switching-state-space followers (Nakamura et al., Raphael, Antescofo, ACCompanion). Neural trackers (RL agents, audio-conditioned U-Nets, CODA's Mamba+CNN cascade) are newer and mostly target sheet-image following. Reported accuracies cluster around 85 to 97 percent of events within 0.5 s and 40 to 65 percent within 100 ms for piano; opera-length tracking is far worse (74 to 94 percent of bars within 1 s).

### Cited Findings
- Dixon's On-Line Time Warping (OLTW) is the foundational online aligner: incremental forward path, linear time and space, with a search width c and a MaxRunCount bound that in Arzt & Widmer's implementation is c = 500 and MaxRunCount = 3, encoding the assumption that a performance will not be more than 3x faster or slower than the reference — [Dixon, IJCAI 2005](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2005/240); [Arzt & Widmer 2010, "Towards Effective 'Any-Time' Music Tracking"](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer's extensions to OLTW include a "backward-forward" strategy that reconsiders past decisions, a simple reactive tempo model that stretches/compresses the reference, and semitone-onset features that keep only the increase in energy per bin to emphasise note onsets — [Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf); [Arzt & Widmer, ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer ISMIR 2015, "Real-time Music Tracking using Multiple Performances as a Reference": standard OLTW on synthesised score got 96 to 99 percent of piano notes within 250 ms but only 71 percent (Mahler 4) and 84 percent (Beethoven 3) of orchestral downbeats; using 7 off-line-aligned real performances as parallel references and taking the median position raised these to 83.7 and 89.6 percent, and errors above 1 s became rare — [Arzt & Widmer, ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf)
- Nakamura, Nakamura & Sagayama, "Real-Time Audio-to-Score Alignment of Music Performances Containing Errors and Arbitrary Repeats and Skips" (arXiv 1512.07748; IEEE/ACM TASLP 24(2), 2016): HMM over score events with constant-Q features (20 ms hop, 128 ms frame), runs in real time on a 3.3 GHz i3 for scores up to about 10^4 events; on clarinet practice data it tracked within 0.7 s on average after repeats/skips and caught up about 20x faster than Antescofo — [arXiv 1512.07748](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.07748); details via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.07748)
- Nakamura et al., "Outer-Product Hidden Markov Model and Polyphonic MIDI Score Following" (arXiv 1404.2313, 2014): for MIDI input, recovers after arbitrary jumps within about 2 chords on average (2.15 / 2.24 / 1.80 chords on Debussy / Mozart / Mussorgsky) versus 3.25 / 3.30 / 1.95 for a uniform-skip model and 6 to 8 chords for a no-skip model; online error rates 3 to 5 percent on play-throughs and 8.7 to 10.1 percent on practice sessions with many mistakes — [arXiv 1404.2313](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)
- Antescofo (Cont, IRCAM) is a coupled duration-focused HMM/semi-Markov architecture with explicit tempo inference and anticipation, used by professional musicians; it is the baseline Nakamura compares against and detected only 4 of 63 repeats/skips in the clarinet practice data — [Cont, IEEE TPAMI 2009, cited in Arzt & Widmer 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf); [IRCAM Antescofo synchronisation docs](https://antescofo-doc.ircam.fr/Reference/time_synchro/); [Nakamura ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.07748)
- Raphael's Music Plus One: "Listen" (HMM score following), "Predict" (Bayesian belief network over note timing), "Play" (phase vocoder on a Music-Minus-One recording); it learns the soloist's rhythmic interpretation from rehearsals — [Raphael, NIPS 2001](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/2b0f658cbffd284984fb11d90254081f-Paper.pdf); [Raphael, ICML 2010](https://icml.cc/imls/conferences/2010/papers/904.pdf)
- The ACCompanion (Cancino-Chacón, Peter, Hu, Karystinaios, Henkel, Foscarin, Varga, Widmer; arXiv 2304.12939; IJCAI 2023): symbolic (MIDI) input; two followers compared, a switching Kalman filter HMM (hidden: score onset, insertion state, tempo; observations: pitches in 10 ms windows and IOIs) and an ensemble of OLTW trackers each aligned to a different pre-recorded reference performance, combined by mean position. OLTW median asynchrony 60.6 ms with 38.0 / 63.3 / 86.7 percent of notes within 25 / 50 / 100 ms; the HMM had median 645.7 ms and "struggles with complex music". No latency figures reported — [arXiv 2304.12939 (HTML v2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12939v2); supplementary material at [cpjku.github.io/accompanion_ijcai2023](https://cpjku.github.io/accompanion_ijcai2023/)
- Matchmaker (Park, Cancino-Chacón, Chiruthapudi, Nam; arXiv 2510.10087; ISMIR 2025): open-source Python library implementing OLTW-Dixon, OLTW-Arzt (Cython, backward-forward) and an attack-steady-release HMM; on (n)ASAP with chroma, OLTW-Arzt reached 44.1 / 58.3 / 84.8 / 92.0 percent of notes within 50 / 100 / 500 / 1000 ms (median error 91 ms), the HMM only 15.6 / 22.2 / 37.5 / 43.8 percent; per-step alignment latency 0.07 ms (OLTW-Arzt), 1.22 ms (OLTW-Dixon), 3.59 ms (HMM); feature latency 0.9 ms (log-spectral energy) to 42.6 ms (CQT) — [arXiv 2510.10087 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087); code [github.com/pymatchmaker/matchmaker](https://github.com/pymatchmaker/matchmaker)
- Peter, Hu & Widmer, "Pairing Real-Time Piano Transcription with Symbol-level Tracking" (arXiv 2505.05078; SMC 2025): streaming transcription (uni-directional LSTM, 32 ms hop, fixed 174 ms latency) feeding a symbol-level OLTW whose cost combines a binary pitch error with a timing error against an extrapolated onset; each DTW cell carries its own tempo estimate ("path-wise tempo", EMA decay 0.1) so no external tempo model is needed. Transcribed-input results: 97.3 / 94.7 / 85.9 / 63.7 percent within 0.5 / 0.25 / 0.1 / 0.05 s with 91.7 percent tracking robustness, versus 95.0 / 89.0 / 71.4 / 49.3 percent for audio-chroma OLTW; onset-sensitive audio features were more precise at tight thresholds but far less robust (58 to 72 percent) — [arXiv 2505.05078 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05078v1)
- Peter, "Online Symbolic Music Alignment with Offline Reinforcement Learning" (arXiv 2401.00466; ISMIR 2023): attention-based agent estimates score position from local score and performance context, trained as offline RL because states can be sampled exhaustively; evaluated as an online note-wise aligner and score follower against an OLTW baseline — [arXiv 2401.00466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00466)
- Learned image-based followers (JKU): Dorfer, Henkel & Widmer framed score following as an RL game over sheet-image windows plus spectrogram excerpts (ISMIR 2018 / TISMIR 2019, arXiv 1807.06391); Henkel et al. built an audio-conditioned U-Net with FiLM layers for position estimation in full sheet images (arXiv 1910.07254) and "Learning to Read and Follow Music in Complete Score Sheet Images" (arXiv 2007.10736, ISMIR 2020) — [arXiv 1807.06391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06391); [arXiv 1910.07254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.07254); [arXiv 2007.10736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10736)
- CODA (Yang, Chen, Han; arXiv 2607.21899; accepted ISMIR 2026): cascaded system-then-bar-then-note prediction on sheet images from a two-layer causal Mamba audio encoder (78-dim log filterbank, about 20 fps) with beam search (k = 3 systems, m = 3 bars); 12.8 ms per frame on an RTX A6000; 91.4 percent of onsets within 0.10 s on synthetic MSMD vs 83.7 percent for the prior CYOLO baseline, 74.3 vs 63.0 percent on real recordings — [arXiv 2607.21899 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21899); code [github.com/ValleyC/CODA](https://github.com/ValleyC/CODA)
- Other 2025-2026 arXiv entries: "Musical Score Following using Statistical Inference" (Cowley, arXiv 2502.10426: Gaussian-process note inference in about 18 ms frames feeding a duration-dependent HMM); "A Neural Score Follower for Computer Accompaniment of Polyphonic Musical Instruments" (Pillay, arXiv 2503.06348, self-described as not yet practical); "JIT-MPE: Just-in-Time Multi-Pitch Estimation under Output Deadlines" (arXiv 2610.33113, causal model refined as 20 to 160 ms of future audio arrives, aimed at score followers); "AIRPiano" (arXiv 2611.30049, Transformer following of tabletop hand gestures) — [arXiv search "score following"](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=%22score+following%22&searchtype=all&order=-announced_date_first&size=50)

### Inferences
- For a lyric-slide follower, the ACCompanion and Matchmaker results argue for OLTW over a plain HMM when the observation stream is noisy: the HMM without a tempo model collapsed (15 to 22 percent within 100 ms) while OLTW with Arzt's backward-forward correction was the most robust and the cheapest per step.
- Every system with sub-100 ms precision uses onset-sensitive features or symbolic onsets; chroma/MFCC-only trackers plateau around 90 to 150 ms median error. Whisper word timestamps alone (0.5 s passes) will not reach 300 ms; a separate onset/phrase-onset stream is what the literature uses for precision.

### Gaps
- No arXiv paper evaluates a follower whose observations are ASR word hypotheses; all lyrics-based trackers use frame-level phoneme posteriograms instead.
- ACCompanion, Peter 2025 and the Brazier opera papers report no end-to-end latency numbers; only Matchmaker, CODA and the transcription-based tracker (174 ms) do.

## Key question 2: How do systems model "the performer may jump to any section at any time", what jump priors do they use, and how do they trade off false jumps against late detection?

### Takeaway
Three design families appear: (a) a global coarse "where could we be" search that proposes hypotheses which fine trackers must then verify over several seconds against the trusted hypothesis (Arzt 2010; Brazier 2021 LR/HR); (b) an HMM whose transition matrix is a local band plus a rank-one "stop x resume" skip term with a tiny per-note skip probability and a resume prior that favours bar and phrase starts (Nakamura 2014/2016); (c) silence-gated widening of the search beam with a grace window (CODA 2026). All three explicitly accept multi-second detection delay in exchange for rare false jumps; the measured costs are median 7 to 15 s to confirm a jump (Arzt), about 0.7 s (Nakamura, monophonic audio) or about 2 chords (Nakamura, MIDI), and 0.72 s mean recovery (CODA, images).

### Cited Findings
- Arzt & Widmer 2010 (two-level "any-time" tracker): a Rough Position Estimator computes, for every new 300 ms low-resolution frame, greedy backward alignments of the last n = 9 s of live audio ending at every possible score position, normalises the costs to [0,1], and passes only positions with similarity at least 0.95 as hypotheses; 3 to 4 parallel high-resolution OLTW matchers are initialised a few seconds in the past at those hypotheses so they do not have to wait for new audio — [Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer 2010 decision rule: only matchers that have tracked at least 5 s of audio are eligible; for each candidate matcher the cumulative sum over its lifetime of (its alignment cost minus the trusted matcher's cost) is compared to two thresholds, reject if below -r*c_t with r = 2 and accept as new trusted if above s*c_t with s = 10, "which gives a good trade-off between fast response times and overall robustness"; the acceptance threshold is doubled when a candidate belongs to the same repetition class as the trusted matcher — [Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer 2010 structure model: a self-similarity matrix over the score's low-resolution features yields an equivalence relation over repeated segments; the system prunes hypotheses equivalent to ones already being checked and applies a configurable strategy such as "prefer the linearly next segment" (concert) or "jump to the nearest hypothesis" (practice). Without it, on the repetition-rich Schubert piece the tracker jumped to a completely wrong position in 28 percent of performances and made musically wrong jumps between equivalent instances in 30 percent; with it both dropped to 0 percent, and overall "a wrong jump or an unnecessary jump roughly occurs only once every 2 hours of a live performance" — [Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer 2010 detection delay: time spent at wrong positions until the system jumps to the correct one, for "starting in the middle" (600 edited performances, 38 h): median 7.2 / 10.5 / 8.7 s (Chopin / Rachmaninov / Schubert), best 3.5 to 5 s, worst 56 to 69 s; for "leaving out parts" (394 performances): median 9.6 / 14.7 / 9.3 s, worst up to 86.8 s; the extra misleading context after a skip slowed the rough estimator, and the authors suggest adaptive thresholds "of course there is a trade-off between detection speed and robustness" — [Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)
- Nakamura et al. 2014 outer-product HMM: transition probability a(i,j) = band(i,j) + S_i * r_j where S_i is the stop probability at chord i and r_j the resume distribution, assumed independent; average skip probability per chord was 0.0263 (band width 4), 0.0098 (width 10) or 0.0048 (width 20); r_j was estimated from practice recordings with 0.01 added per bin, and resume points clustered at bar onsets and phrase beginnings while stop points were spread out; backward skips were more common than forward skips and far skips rarer than near ones — [arXiv 1404.2313 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)
- Nakamura 2014 complexity trick: the band-plus-rank-one structure reduces Viterbi updates from O(N^2) to O(DN) with no pruning, keeping updates within tens of milliseconds up to about 10,000 chords; a theoretical following time L_FT ~ H'(r)/h_eff + 1 shows that a lower-entropy resume prior shortens recovery — [arXiv 1404.2313 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)
- Nakamura 2014 false-jump failure mode: "a note in a neighbouring chord may be matched to a remote chord, and the algorithm may then lock onto the wrong position"; cross-validated priors helped less than closed-data priors, so wrong jump priors limit the gain — [arXiv 1404.2313 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)
- Nakamura et al. 2016 (audio): uniform resume prior r_i = 1/N; per-event stop probability s_j swept from 10^-1 to 10^-5000; detection of the 63 annotated repeats/skips in clarinet practice data stayed between 55 and 60 for s_j from 10^-1 down to 10^-1000 (so the method is insensitive over that range), fell to 43 at 10^-5000 and 13 at s_j = 0 (Antescofo: 4). Larger s_j raised false alarms because insertions/deletions/substitutions were read as jumps; accuracy fell for s_j above about 10^-500 on Bach10. An explicit "break" state (self-transition 0.996) modelling pauses cut misdetections by about 1.2x. The decoder has no confirmation delay: it outputs argmax of the filtered posterior each 20 ms frame and relies on full (unpruned) search to recover if wrong — [arXiv 1512.07748 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.07748)
- Brazier & Widmer, "Handling Structural Mismatches in Real-time Opera Tracking" (arXiv 2105.08531; EUSIPCO 2020): high-resolution tracker is a forward-only JumpDTW (100 fps MFCC, 40 s context) in which each part-start can inherit cumulative cost from any part-end but only 8 transitions are kept (repeat current part, continue, or jump to one of the next 6 parts), activated when the position passes the part end minus half the context window; a low-resolution tracker (600 ms Hann window, 300 ms hop, cosine distance over the last 30 frames, about 9 s) considers all score positions and may start anywhere. It redirects the HR tracker only when a reliability flag is set (the last 30 LR positions fit a line with slope in [0.5, 1.5]) and the HR position lies outside the LR interval; the HR cost matrix is then reset to +inf everywhere except the redirected point — [arXiv 2105.08531 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.08531)
- Brazier 2021 results on 20 versions of two live Don Giovanni performances with one to two thirds of parts removed: part-wise accuracy rose from 10.4 percent (baseline OLTW) and 28.3 percent (JumpDTW alone) to 79.5 percent (baseline + LR redirect) and 84.0 percent (JumpDTW + LR); the LR tracker alone was 98.9 percent part-correct when its reliability flag was set, which covered 63.5 percent of the opera. Detection delay in seconds is not reported — [arXiv 2105.08531 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.08531)
- CODA 2026 break mode: waveform energy with hysteresis thresholds 0.1 and 0.25 (normalised), entry requires 3 consecutive low-energy frames (about 150 ms); on entry the committed position is frozen, learned transition penalties are suppressed and the beam widens to all systems on the page; on re-entry penalties stay suppressed for an 8-frame (about 400 ms) grace window while the model relocalises, with the Mamba state preserved across the gap. Transition penalties are learned but clamped non-positive and bounded so audio evidence can override them. On 131 real repeat jumps: system-level recovery within 1 s / 2 s of 78 / 91 percent and 0.72 s mean recovery latency (CYOLO baseline 12 / 20 percent, 4.31 s); on 84 random jumps 64 / 80 percent and 1.24 s; without break mode recovery fell to 29 / 44 percent and 2.63 s on repeats — [arXiv 2607.21899 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21899)
- Shan & Tsai, "Improved Handling of Repeats and Jumps in Audio-Sheet Image Synchronization" (arXiv 2007.14580; ISMIR 2020), offline but instructive: Hierarchical DTW does a second dynamic program at the segment (sheet-line) level; starting on any line costs nothing, moving to the next line costs 1, staying or skipping one line costs 0.5, backward jumps are allowed only to lines already visited, forward jumps only to the "leading edge" one line beyond the furthest seen, and each jump gets a bonus of -gamma * average line score (gamma = 1, "roughly one line of matching music") so a jump must be paid for by evidence. Accuracy at 0.5 s collar: 83.9 / 82.8 / 82.4 / 81.6 percent on 1 / 2 / 3 repeats / D.S. al fine versus 71.5 to 71.8 percent for Jump DTW — [arXiv 2007.14580 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.14580); code [github.com/HMC-MIR/YoutubeScoreFollowing](https://github.com/HMC-MIR/YoutubeScoreFollowing)
- Agrawal, Wolff & Dixon, "Structure-Aware Audio-to-Score Alignment using Progressively Dilated CNNs" (arXiv 2102.00382; ICASSP 2021) and the follow-ups (arXiv 2204.08822, arXiv 2206.00454) train on synthetic performance-score pairs containing repeats and jumps; they are offline alignment models, not online followers — [arXiv 2102.00382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00382); [arXiv 2204.08822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.08822); [arXiv 2206.00454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00454)
- ACCompanion lesson on premature movement: after a pause the OLTW follower "frantically" aligned notes where there were none and the tempo ran away; the authors also warn that very high predictive accuracy can mean the model follows the input too tightly, and that the system needs an explicit model of when to follow and when to lead — [arXiv 2304.12939 (HTML v2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12939v2)

### Inferences
- The concrete design patterns that recur across these papers map directly onto a slide follower:
  1. Two-level architecture: a cheap global "where could we be" scorer over all sections (coarse features, several-second window) that only proposes; a fine tracker per hypothesis that must accumulate evidence before it is trusted (Arzt 2010, Brazier 2021).
  2. Cumulative-margin confirmation rather than instantaneous argmax: accept a competitor only when its summed cost advantage over the trusted hypothesis exceeds a large multiple (s = 10) of the current cost, reject when it falls behind by a smaller multiple (r = 2); minimum evidence duration (5 s) before any decision (Arzt 2010). Nakamura's argmax-per-frame decoder, by contrast, has no confirmation and tolerates false jumps via rapid recovery, which is acceptable for accompaniment audio but not for a visible slide change.
  3. Rank-one jump prior: keep the local band transitions plus a single tiny "stop anywhere, resume according to r" term; put r's mass on section and phrase starts (chorus start, verse start), and make backward jumps (repeat a chorus) more probable than forward skips, since that is what performers were observed to do (Nakamura 2014).
  4. Reliability gate on the coarse tracker: redirect only when the coarse estimate has been linearly consistent (slope 0.5 to 1.5) for a window (about 9 s) and disagrees with the fine tracker (Brazier 2021).
  5. Structure-equivalence handling: when a section's text/music is identical to another's (repeated chorus), do not treat a competitor at an equivalent position as a jump at all; prefer "linearly next" for a scripted service, "nearest" for a rehearsal (Arzt 2010).
  6. Silence/pause as the jump-permission signal: widen the search only after sustained silence (3 frames, about 150 ms, with hysteresis) and keep it widened for a short grace window (about 400 ms) after sound resumes; otherwise keep transition penalties on (CODA 2026). For worship sets, inter-section instrumental gaps are exactly these windows.
  7. Jump cost that must be "paid for" by roughly one segment of matching evidence (Shan & Tsai gamma = 1).
  8. Ensemble/median of parallel trackers against several reference performances to suppress single-tracker runaways (Arzt 2015, ACCompanion).
- The measured trade-off is stark: Arzt's conservative thresholds give almost no false jumps (one per 2 h) at a median 7 to 15 s detection delay; CODA's silence-gated approach reaches 0.7 s mean recovery but was evaluated only on piano with clean section boundaries. A slide follower that must "almost never move early" should default to conservative confirmation and use silence/instrumental gaps to lower the confirmation requirement locally.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a false-jump rate per hour except Arzt 2010; Brazier and CODA report accuracy only.
- No online system was found that learns the jump prior r_j from the same performer's previous services; Nakamura estimated it from practice recordings offline.

## Key question 3: Which approaches work from audio alone without a symbolic score, aligning against a reference recording or a text/phoneme sequence?

### Takeaway
Reference-recording alignment is well established: Arzt & Widmer 2015 tracked a full symphony with no symbolic score by annotating one recording and auto-aligning six more; Brazier & Widmer 2021 and Park et al. 2024 align live singing to a phoneme posteriogram or chroma+phoneme features of a reference recording whose lyrics were annotated offline. Lyrics-only tracking is unreliable during instrumental passages, so the best systems hand off between a music tracker and a lyrics tracker.

### Cited Findings
- Arzt & Widmer 2015 concert deployment (Concertgebouw, 7 Feb 2015, Alpensinfonie): no symbolic score was available; one performance was manually annotated at the downbeat level and 6 more aligned to it offline, giving 7 OLTW agents whose median position drove synchronised visuals over WiFi to about 30 tablets; post-hoc evaluation gave 81.8 / 93.2 / 98.0 percent of downbeats within 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 s for the multi-agent tracker versus 78.3 / 92.2 / 97.5 percent for a single reference — [Arzt & Widmer, ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf)
- Brazier & Widmer, "On-Line Audio-to-Lyrics Alignment Based on a Reference Performance" (arXiv 2107.14496; ISMIR 2021): a CP-ResNet acoustic model (80 MFCCs at 16 kHz, 10 ms hop; receptive field 57 frames so output delay 28 frames = 280 ms; one 60-dim vector per 40 ms over 57 phonemes of English/German/French/Spanish/Italian plus space, instrumental and CTC blank) trained with CTC on DALI (259 h, no vocal separation). The reference recording is run through the same model and linked to lyrics by manual word/sentence annotations; OLTW with cosine distance over an 8,000-vector (320 s) window, skipping blank tokens, aligns live and reference posteriograms. On Don Giovanni mean error fell from 1,915 ms (music-only baseline) to 818 to 846 ms with 80.5 percent of annotations within 1 s; on Jingju (language unseen) from 2,943 ms to 810 ms with 89 percent within 1 s. The posteriogram trackers needed no silence detector, whereas both music baselines did — [arXiv 2107.14496 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.14496)
- Brazier & Widmer, "Improving Real-time Score Following in Opera by Combining Music with Lyrics Tracking" (arXiv 2110.02592; NLP4MusA 2021): each opera part is labelled by a voice-over-music ratio from voice/music detectors on the reference; the music tracker leads, and when it enters a voice-dominant part the lyrics tracker is started with its cost vector set to +inf everywhere except 0 at the music tracker's position, after which only the lyrics tracker's position is used until it reaches a music-dominant part. Hard handoff, no confidence weighting. Mean bar error on Don Giovanni fell to 335 ms (Fischer) and 410 ms (Manacorda) with 94.1 / 91.6 percent of bars within 1 s, versus 811 / 561 ms for music-only OLTW. Compensating for the 280 ms model delay by offsetting the output made results worse, so the delay was ignored. The lyrics tracker is "entirely lost during instrumental parts" — [arXiv 2110.02592 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.02592)
- Park, Yong, Kwon & Nam, "A Real-Time Lyrics Alignment System Using Chroma and Phonetic Features for Classical Vocal Performance" (arXiv 2401.09200; ICASSP 2024): offline phase aligns a reference recording to a MIDI-synthesised score with MrMsDTW (synctoolbox, chroma + DLNCO) to get word/line/note-level lyric times; online phase is a reimplementation of Dixon's OLTW at 25 fps (40 ms hop, 16 kHz) with a 3 s window and 160 ms input buffers. Phonetic posteriorgram from a CRNN trained on TIMIT; the best online setup was chroma + a 5-class phoneme posteriorgram. On the 24-song winterreise_rt set (voice-note level): offline 83.4 / 93.3 percent of onsets within 300 / 500 ms; online chroma + Phoneme5 75.5 / 85.8 percent within 300 / 500 ms (mean abs error 376 ms, median 136 ms); chroma-only 69.4 / 79.2 percent (mean 1,366 ms); PPG-only (39 phonemes) 37.3 / 43.4 percent. Dataset and build script at github.com/laurenceyoon/winterreise_rt; no system code released — [arXiv 2401.09200 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.09200); [winterreise_rt repo](https://github.com/laurenceyoon/winterreise_rt)
- Whisper streaming for word-level text hypotheses: Macháček, Dabre & Bojar, "Turning Whisper into Real-Time Transcription System" (arXiv 2307.14743) confirm output with LocalAgreement-2 (longest common prefix of two consecutive hypothesis updates) and report about 3.3 s average latency for English on ESIC on an A40 GPU; WhisperKit (arXiv 2507.10860) uses the same policy, describing unconfirmed hypothesis text as low-latency with occasional later corrections — [arXiv 2307.14743](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14743); [ufal/whisper_streaming](https://github.com/ufal/whisper_streaming); [arXiv 2507.10860](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10860)
- No arXiv paper from 2022 to 2026 was found on real-time lyrics tracking for popular-music or karaoke vocals; all published real-time lyrics trackers target classical vocal music and opera — [search result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09200)

### Inferences
- The reference-recording approach (align live audio to a previous recording of the same song whose slides were timed once) is the most mature audio-only route and directly matches a worship context where the band's own prior recordings exist; Arzt 2015 showed it beats synthesised references even when the references are by different performers.
- Frame-level phoneme posteriograms (40 ms, 280 ms delay) are a fundamentally lower-latency text signal than Whisper word hypotheses (0.5 s passes, multi-second confirmation); a slide follower that needs 300 ms timing should treat Whisper words as the coarse "which section" evidence and a frame-level stream (phoneme posteriors, onsets, or chroma against the reference) as the fine timing signal.
- Hard music/lyrics handoff by section type (Brazier 2021) is a workable pattern for worship sets: instrumental intros and interludes are tracked by audio-to-reference, sung sections by text.

### Gaps
- None of the lyrics trackers evaluates repeated or skipped sections; opera and Winterreise evaluations assume the performed structure matches the reference.
- Latency of the Park et al. pipeline end to end is not reported (only hop and buffer sizes).

## Key question 4: What onset / beat / phrase-boundary detection methods time transitions precisely (within 300 ms) under live conditions?

### Takeaway
Within score following, precision comes from onset-emphasising features (energy increase per semitone bin, log-spectral energy) and from symbolic onsets via streaming transcription (174 to 380 ms fixed latency). Standalone streaming beat/downbeat trackers now run under 50 ms latency (BEAST) or at zero latency (real-time PLP). No vocal-phrase-onset detector designed for live singing was found on arXiv.

### Cited Findings
- Arzt & Widmer's high-resolution features: 46 ms Hamming window, 20 ms hop, 84 semitone-spaced bins, storing only the positive energy increase per bin "to emphasize note onsets, which are the most important indicators of musical timing" — [Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)
- Matchmaker: log-spectral energy (onset-sensitive STFT feature) gave the lowest median error (241.9 ms) and lowest feature latency (0.91 ms) of all tested features, ahead of chroma (265.5 ms, 3.05 ms) — [arXiv 2510.10087 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087)
- Peter 2025: onset-based audio features (LNSO, LNCO) were the most precise at 25 to 50 ms thresholds but dropped robustness to 58 to 72 percent; the streaming transcription front end has a fixed 174 ms latency that the authors compensate by predicting onsets 174 ms ahead, at a small precision cost — [arXiv 2505.05078 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05078v1)
- Streaming piano transcription with consistent onset/offset decoding reports 380 ms latency for a CNN encoder using 19 future frames — [arXiv 2503.01362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01362); a 2025 study of minimum-latency piano transcription notes that regression onset targets rise several frames before the true onset, which a causal model cannot predict without lookahead — [arXiv 2509.07586](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07586)
- BEAST (Chang & Su; arXiv 2312.17156; ICASSP 2024): streaming Transformer with contextual block processing and relative positional encoding for online joint beat/downbeat tracking; F1 80.04 percent (beat) and 46.78 percent (downbeat) with maximum latency under 50 ms, about 5 points over the prior online state of the art — [arXiv 2312.17156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17156)
- Meier, Chiu & Müller, real-time predominant local pulse (PLP) beat tracking (TISMIR 2024, vol. 7): adapts PLP to run at zero latency and offers optional lookahead giving beat context, beat stability and beat lookahead for predicting beats in advance — [AudioLabs ePrint](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.com/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/03_publications/2024_MeierCM_RealTimePLP_TISMIR_ePrint.pdf)
- Brazier & Widmer 2020 used three identical one-layer LSTM detectors (55 cells, about 1.1 s context; 100 ms window, 20 ms hop) for applause, music and speech; a detector must fire for more than 400 ms, and only within 1 s of a scene transition, before it forces the tracker to the transition point; a speech-based rule halts the tracker at a vocal section until the first voice activity in the live signal, which "synchronises the start of the vocal section". Adding all three detectors cut Act 1 mean error from 9.6 s to 0.3 s and raised bars within 1 s from 74 to 94 percent — [arXiv 2006.11033 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11033)
- Low-latency state-space voice activity detection (arXiv 2609.11110) targets onset-time accuracy for streaming VAD and argues onset latency is hard to measure because annotation timestamps are noisy — [arXiv 2609.11110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11110)
- CODA uses waveform-energy hysteresis (0.1 / 0.25 normalised) with a 3-frame (about 150 ms) persistence requirement as its silence/resume detector at about 20 fps — [arXiv 2607.21899 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21899)

### Inferences
- Brazier's "halt at the vocal section boundary, release on first voice activity" is the closest published mechanism to a phrase-onset-timed slide change: the follower parks at the predicted boundary and a cheap detector (VAD / voice onset) fires the transition, which removes tracker lag from the timing path.
- The 400 ms minimum-duration and 1 s proximity gates are the published anti-premature-trigger values; a slide follower aiming at 300 ms would need a shorter persistence requirement (CODA's 150 ms) combined with a position prior that already expects the boundary.

### Gaps
- No arXiv paper measures vocal phrase-onset detection latency in a live PA/room environment; all onset results are for piano or beat tracking.
- No paper combines a VAD/onset trigger with a word-level ASR follower.

## Key question 5: What self-adaptation or learning-from-rehearsal methods exist (tempo, pronunciation, acoustic models from previous performances)?

### Takeaway
Learning from rehearsal is a long-standing idea (Raphael's Music Plus One learns the soloist's timing; Arzt uses prior performances as references and as tempo models; Nakamura estimates jump priors from practice recordings; ACCompanion derives tempo expectations from reference performances), but all of it is offline between performances, not within one. No published follower adapts its acoustic or pronunciation model online.

### Cited Findings
- Raphael's Music Plus One learns "a performer-specific model for the rhythmic interpretation of the composition from rehearsals"; the Predict stage is a Bayesian belief network over note timing that is retuned from rehearsal data so the accompaniment anticipates the soloist — [Raphael, NIPS 2001](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/2b0f658cbffd284984fb11d90254081f-Paper.pdf); [Raphael, ICML 2010](https://icml.cc/imls/conferences/2010/papers/904.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer, "Simple Tempo Models for Real-time Music Tracking" (SMC 2010): tempo models are learned automatically from many different interpretations of a piece, possibly by many performers, and used to stretch/compress the reference during tracking — [Arzt & Widmer SMC 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_SMC_2010.pdf)
- Arzt & Widmer 2015: prior performances are aligned offline to the score and used directly as references, which "implicitly encodes a lot of information that is missing in the symbolic score", including local tempo, loudness and articulation; the reference set was chosen manually to have the same structure as the piece to be tracked, with automatic structure handling left to future work — [Arzt & Widmer, ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf)
- Nakamura 2014 estimated the resume distribution r_j and local transition statistics (0.845 next-chord, 0.113 self-transition) from practice recordings; piece-specific and performer-specific repetition habits changed the distributions, and mismatched priors (cross-validation) reduced the benefit — [arXiv 1404.2313 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)
- ACCompanion: the Linear-Tempo-Expectation (LTE) model adds a tempo expectation derived from reference performances and achieved the lowest onset prediction error (23.3 ms) versus 81.9 ms for the plain linear sensorimotor model and 1,154 ms for a Kalman tempo filter, with the caveat that the reference was the test performance plus noise; the OLTW follower aligns to pre-recorded references "ideally by the same player"; model hyperparameters were set by grid search over about 800 combinations, not adapted online — [arXiv 2304.12939 (HTML v2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12939v2)
- Brazier & Widmer 2021 lyrics tracker: an Italian-only acoustic model (10.8 h) diverged on all alignments, while the multilingual and English models worked even on Chinese Jingju; the authors plan to replace manual reference annotation with offline alignment — [arXiv 2107.14496 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.14496)
- Park et al. 2024 derive all lyric timings for the reference recording offline with MrMsDTW, so a new reference recording can be annotated automatically before a performance — [arXiv 2401.09200 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.09200)

### Inferences
- The transferable recipe is: after each service, align the recording to the slide sequence offline, add it to the per-song reference pool (Arzt 2015 median-of-trackers), and re-estimate per-song section-transition statistics (Nakamura's r_j) from where the band actually went; this is cheap and does not require any online learning.
- Reference-performance tempo expectation (ACCompanion LTE) is the published way to predict phrase starts ahead of time, which is what a slide must do to land within 300 ms.

### Gaps
- No paper adapts a Whisper/ASR model or pronunciation lexicon to a specific singer from rehearsal; nothing found on arXiv.
- No quantitative study of how many prior performances are needed before the reference-pool approach saturates (Arzt used 7 by availability).

## Key question 6: Which approaches have public code?

### Takeaway
Public code exists for the two most recent OLTW/HMM toolkits (Matchmaker) and two structure-aware trackers (CODA online, HMC Hierarchical DTW offline), plus the Whisper streaming policy and a real-time lyrics evaluation dataset; the JKU opera trackers, Nakamura's HMMs, and the ACCompanion (promised) have no released code found.

### Cited Findings
- Matchmaker real-time alignment library (OLTW-Dixon, OLTW-Arzt, HMM; evaluation metrics): [github.com/pymatchmaker/matchmaker](https://github.com/pymatchmaker/matchmaker) per [arXiv 2510.10087](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087) (software license not stated in the paper; unverified)
- CODA online image-based follower with silence-driven break mode: [github.com/ValleyC/CODA](https://github.com/ValleyC/CODA) per [arXiv 2607.21899](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21899)
- Hierarchical DTW for repeats/jumps (offline), code, data and videos: [github.com/HMC-MIR/YoutubeScoreFollowing](https://github.com/HMC-MIR/YoutubeScoreFollowing) per [arXiv 2007.14580](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.14580)
- winterreise_rt real-time lyrics alignment evaluation set and build script: [github.com/laurenceyoon/winterreise_rt](https://github.com/laurenceyoon/winterreise_rt); demo site [laurenceyoon.github.io/real-time-lyrics-alignment](https://laurenceyoon.github.io/real-time-lyrics-alignment/) per [arXiv 2401.09200](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.09200)
- Whisper streaming with LocalAgreement: [github.com/ufal/whisper_streaming](https://github.com/ufal/whisper_streaming) per [arXiv 2307.14743](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14743)
- ACCompanion: paper says the system "will also be published as open source software" and points to [cpjku.github.io/accompanion_ijcai2023](https://cpjku.github.io/accompanion_ijcai2023/); no repository URL appears in the paper (unverified whether released since) — [arXiv 2304.12939](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12939v2)
- Peter 2023 RL aligner lists a Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.10265367 whose contents the abstract page does not describe (uncertain whether code or data) — [arXiv 2401.00466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00466)
- No code found for: Brazier & Widmer opera trackers (2006.11033, 2105.08531, 2107.14496, 2110.02592), Nakamura HMMs (1404.2313, 1512.07748; only a demo video), Peter 2025 symbol-level tracker (uses Matchmaker for its audio baseline), Arzt & Widmer 2010/2015, Henkel full-page followers (2007.10736) — per the respective arXiv pages cited above
- Antescofo is distributed by IRCAM as a Max/Pd object with documentation, not as open source — [IRCAM Antescofo user guide](https://support.ircam.fr/docs/Antescofo/manuals/UserGuide/intro/)

### Inferences
- Matchmaker is the practical starting point for an audio-to-reference OLTW layer (it already implements Arzt's backward-forward variant with 0.07 ms per step), while CODA's energy-hysteresis break mode and Arzt's two-threshold decision maker would need to be reimplemented from the paper descriptions.

### Gaps
- Repository licenses and current maintenance status were not verified in this pass.
