# Ship FreeShow Jumps Before Smarter Listening

**Voxxa should move its live output from WorshipTools Presenter to FreeShow, and it should do that before it spends more effort on recognition, because Voxxa's own numbers and the published literature agree that the follower already knows *which* slide is wanted far more often than Presenter lets it *show* that slide.** On seven labelled solo-vocal takes the parts-map follower got **74 of 77 changes right with 4 wrong moves and one move more than 1 s early**, and the onset trigger cut median delay on the founder's take of *It Is Well* from **1.90 s to 0.18 s**, yet the one mechanically successful live pass (live4) still could not return to a chorus, because Presenter offers only Right and Left keystrokes, no section or slide-number command, no API and no read-back ([brief §2–4](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)) ([WorshipTools shortcuts](https://www.worshiptools.com/en-gb/docs/182-keyboard-shortcuts)). FreeShow is free, GPL-3.0, Windows-native, and exposes `index_select_slide`, `id_select_group`, `get_output_slide_text` and `get_output_group_name` over a local socket, with a Companion module and a stage display that already shows the next slide ([FreeShow API](https://freeshow.app/api)). The ranked path is therefore: (1) prove and adopt the FreeShow jump driver with read-back; (2) finish ADR-031's parts map and add the one gate the score-following canon says it lacks, a silence-gated jump prior estimated per worship leader; (3) keep Whisper as the coarse "which part" witness and move all timing to the onset detector plus a frame-level aligner, since Whisper's own timestamps carry **0.82 s** error against **0.071 s** for a CTC head on the same encoder ([Wang et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.12488)); (4) put the lab's input chain into the product and obtain a vocal-only desk feed; (5) build the Listener DB as hindsight forced alignment of rehearsal captures, which is how every rehearsal-learning system in the literature works; (6) convert the volunteer role into a "minder" with a reserved hold/kill key, per-song rehearsal gating and a ten-minute training card. Acceptance should use mir_eval's percentage-correct and correct-segments metrics (wrong-slide seconds is one minus PCS times duration) and Matchmaker's multi-window alignment rate; a song is promoted to autonomous only after a rehearsal pass shows zero early moves beyond 0.3 s, zero spurious presses and at least 95% of changes within 1.5 s, and the church runs without a volunteer only after four consecutive shadow Sundays meet that bar. The largest unknowns are FreeShow's undocumented index base and group-id semantics (open question W1), whether the 2 GB GPU can host an aligner beside Whisper, and how strictly to count early moves, given that karaoke listeners tolerate lyrics about 0.3 s early but notice them 0.2 s late.

## Presenter's keystroke ceiling, not recognition, is the first blocker

The brief ranks "jumping in the presentation app" as the top open problem, and the landscape survey shows why no amount of follower work removes it. Presenter's documented shortcuts are Right, Space or PgDn for the next slide, Left or PgUp for the previous, Up and Down for the service item, and F10–F12 for clears; there is no section key, no slide-number key, no remote-control API and no read-back, and a song opens on a leading blank so that Right from a song's last slide drops into the next item's blank ([brief §4](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)) ([WorshipTools shortcuts](https://www.worshiptools.com/en-gb/docs/182-keyboard-shortcuts)). Users have asked WorshipTools for a REST API, MIDI output and a Stream Deck plugin in a thread open since **July 2024**; one built an HTTP-to-MIDI bridge because "Presenter only accepts MIDI input", and no staff reply appears ([WorshipTools help thread](https://www.worshiptools.com/help/thread/6250445659963392)). The MIDI mapping document does not even enumerate which commands are mappable ([WorshipTools MIDI mapping](https://www.worshiptools.com/en-gb/docs/189-midi-mapping)), so the brief's binding profile, which allows only Right and Left and counts positions per key, is already the ceiling of what Presenter permits. Going back to a chorus is N Left presses with every in-between slide shown, and after a failed key sequence the follower's internal position drifts with nothing to check it against, which is exactly the freeze condition ADR-025 §2 describes.

FreeShow removes each of those limits with a documented action API. It listens on WebSocket port 5505 and REST port 5506 with bearer auth, offers `index_select_slide {showId, layoutId, index}`, `name_select_slide`, `id_select_group {id}`, `next_slide`, `previous_slide`, `clear_slide` and `restore_output`, and returns state through `get_output`, `get_output_slide_text`, `get_output_group_name`, `get_slide {showId:"active"}` and `get_groups` ([FreeShow API](https://freeshow.app/api)). The source confirms that non-getter actions return 204 and that getters are polled for up to 10 s ([FreeShow api.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChurchApps/FreeShow/main/src/electron/utils/api.ts)), so the driver should treat every jump as fire-then-verify, calling `get_output_group_name` after the move rather than trusting the call. That read-back is what turns problem #6's "position drift" into a detectable, recoverable state, and because the jump travels over HTTP rather than synthesized keystrokes, the front-window guard that live3 tripped on stops being a safety dependency for jumps at all. FreeShow's MIDI "go to group" semantics (trigger the group, or the next matching group if one is already selected) are the repeat-chorus behaviour a follower needs ([FreeShow MIDI docs](https://freeshow.app/docs/midi)); the Companion module gives a volunteer a one-press surface on the same port ([FreeShow Companion docs](https://freeshow.app/docs/companion)); stage layouts show the current and next slide text ([FreeShow Stage docs](https://freeshow.app/docs/stage)); and Planning Center services download a week ahead ([FreeShow Integrations](https://freeshow.app/docs/integrations)). Voxxa's existing `freeshow.json` binding already wires `goto.item` through `index_select_project_item` then `index_select_slide` and keeps only show id, layout and index from `get_output` ([brief §4](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)).

Three things remain unproven and should be the first ten minutes of work. The docs do not state whether `index` is zero- or one-based, do not say whether `id_select_group.id` takes an internal UUID or a group name such as a chorus label, and do not document getter response shapes ([FreeShow API](https://freeshow.app/api)); the brief's W1 adds that the public API puts fields at the top level while the driver nests them under `data`, so `index_select_slide` may be ignoring its index. The driver is therefore correctly marked UNVERIFIED and runs as a hint until its contract test passes (ADR-024 §5). The honest comparison is that the brief's cheaper FA-44 options are worth their ten-minute tests but cannot win: hide-while-stepping (F10, arrows, show) may or may not keep Presenter clear, and even if it does it leaves no read-back; UI-automation clicks need the same fragile front-window guard. Among the alternatives, Holyrics is the only other free app with a jump by section description (`ActionGoToSlideDescription {name}`) and a `GetCurrentPresentation` read-back ([Holyrics API](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/holyrics/API-Server/main/README-en.md)), and it is the right fallback if FreeShow's group ids prove awkward; ProPresenter 7 has a 0-based slide trigger and a group trigger but is paid per seat ([ProPresenter Companion HELP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitfocus/companion-module-renewedvision-propresenter-api/main/companion/HELP.md)); OpenLP exposes only `POST /show {id}` with no section selector ([OpenLP controller.py](https://gitlab.com/openlp/openlp/-/raw/master/openlp/core/api/versions/v2/controller.py)).

The migration cost sits almost entirely in the song library. WorshipTools has no bulk export; as late as November 2025 the only out-paths were per-song ChordPro export or using OpenLP as an intermediary, which WorshipTools staff themselves recommended for another migration ([WorshipTools thread](https://worshiptools.com/help/thread/6276188217540608)) ([WorshipTools thread](https://www.worshiptools.com/help/thread/6325408358203392)). Since every Voxxa song is already licence-checked and imported from CC, public-domain or church-owned sources (ADR-026), re-importing from those sources into FreeShow is the clean path, and the fix to the `.txt` importer that drops sections (plan V5) becomes doubly important because FreeShow groups are what `id_select_group` targets. The recommendation is a staged move: install FreeShow on the booth PC beside Presenter, run it as the target of `--shadow` during Sunday services while Presenter remains the live output, measure VRAM and CPU with both open (Presenter alone already leaves Whisper at **814 of 2048 MB**), prove W1 and the group-id semantics, then switch the live output for one rehearsed song at a time. The competitor scan reinforces that this is open ground rather than a solved market: the only shipping product that drives an existing presenter from live vocals is macOS-and-ProPresenter-only and warns it "will occasionally fire the wrong slide" ([Lyrics Follower](https://lyricsfollower.efectivduo.com/)), the two open-source projects are standalone or simulation-only ([church-slides](https://github.com/martinmain93/church-slides)) ([lyric-pilot](https://github.com/stephenchenn/lyric-pilot)), and nothing targets Presenter or FreeShow.

## The parts map already matches the score-following canon; it lacks a silence-gated jump prior

ADR-031's design is, point for point, the structure the real-time score-following literature arrived at after a decade of opera and piano tracking, which is the strongest evidence that the founder's option A is right. Brazier and Widmer's high-resolution tracker permits jumps only at known part boundaries and only into part starts, keeping eight transitions (repeat current, continue, or one of the next six parts), while a coarse tracker proposes positions and is believed only when its own trajectory has had a plausible slope for about 9 s ([Brazier & Widmer 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.08531)); Voxxa's rule that a part is entered at its first slide and that leaving the plan needs a 1.5-word margin in two consecutive windows is the same two-level, confirm-before-jump shape. Arzt and Widmer's structure model, which treats repeated segments as an equivalence class and prefers "the linearly next segment" in concert, cut wrong jumps on a repetition-rich Schubert piece from **28% of performances to 0%** and left "a wrong jump or an unnecessary jump roughly once every 2 hours" ([Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)); Voxxa's look-alike rule (never jump between slides sharing ≥70% of words, and skip to one only after time to sing the words in between) and its planned-next handicap of 0 are that equivalence class and that preference. The lab table shows the design paying off: across seven sung takes the parts map scored **74 right changes, 4 wrong moves, 1 move more than 1 s early and 112 wrong-slide seconds**, against **49, 31, 14 and 327** for the plan-order follower, and on the deliberately re-cut flows (extra chorus, skipped verse, chorus first, repeated last line) it recovered 12, 8, 10 and 12 of 14, 8, 10 and 12 changes where the plan-order follower managed 9, 4, 0 and 9 ([brief §2](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)).

What the canon adds is a principled answer to *when* a jump should be cheap. Nakamura, Nakamura and Sagayama found that in practice recordings **59 of 63 repeats and skips followed a break longer than 500 ms**, built an explicit silent "break" state so that a jump is a two-step transition through silence, and measured about 1.2× fewer misdetections with it; their factorised stop-times-resume transition keeps all jumps at linear cost, and their resume distribution, estimated from practice data, put its mass on bar and phrase starts, with backward jumps more common than forward and near jumps more common than far ([Nakamura et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1512.07748)) ([Nakamura et al. 2014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)). CODA's 2026 follower operationalises the same idea for a neural tracker: a waveform-energy detector with hysteresis thresholds of 0.1 and 0.25, entry after three low-energy frames (about 150 ms), a frozen position and a widened beam during the break, and a 400 ms grace window after sound resumes; with break mode it recovered from **78% of real repeat jumps within 1 s and 91% within 2 s** (mean 0.72 s), and without it only 29% and 44% ([CODA 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21899)). Shan and Tsai add that a jump should be "paid for" by roughly one segment of matching evidence before it is accepted ([Shan & Tsai 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.14580)).

Voxxa already computes the break signal: the onset detector in `predict.py` requires at least 200 ms below floor + 5 dB before a level above floor + 10 dB counts as an onset ([brief §1](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)). Exposing that "≥200 ms below floor" event to the parts map as a `break` flag, lowering the leave-the-plan handicaps for a grace window after it and raising them mid-phrase, is a change of a few lines inside the existing budget and it targets live4's remaining errors directly: the two onset fires after a chorus on a repeated closing phrase (−2.7 s and −5.0 s) and the move between two look-alike chorus slides all happened mid-phrase, where a break-gated cost would have held ([brief §2](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)). The hand-set handicaps (planned next 0, chorus −0.5, same part −1.0, other −1.5) are a resume distribution in disguise; Nakamura's finding that cross-validated priors helped less than closed-data priors means the gain comes from estimating them per leader from that leader's own detours, which is step 5's job. The trade-off the papers quantify should set expectations: Arzt's conservative thresholds bought near-zero false jumps at a median detection delay of **7 to 15 s**, while CODA's break-gated search reached 0.7 s only on piano with clean section boundaries ([Arzt & Widmer 2010](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Arzt_Widmer_STAIRS_2010.pdf)) ([CODA 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21899)). Voxxa's 2-window rule, 2 s elimination and 8 s review hold sit between those, and the brief's known cost (the verse after a chorus appearing 1–3 s after its first word) is the price of that position. Generalisation is the open risk the brief names: every threshold was tuned on one highly repetitive hymn and re-cuts that share the singer and room, so the ≥3 real detour takes (V3d task 4) and the V6 held-out corpus decide whether the 1.5-word margin, the 0.7 look-alike cut and the handicaps transfer. On the band song P1 the parts map lost to the plan-order follower (8 versus 11 right) and the brief rightly keeps plan order there; that result should not drive tuning until step 4 delivers a vocal-only feed, because the onset detector found only 2 of 11 starts on the band mix.

## Whisper decides which part; onsets and a frame-level aligner decide when

Voxxa's own measurements reproduce the literature's central lesson about Whisper: words alone cannot time a slide. With 1 s passes the words-only follower placed changes a median **1.90 s** late on T1 and **2.12 s** on T2, and the onset trigger brought those to **0.18 s and 0.04 s**, with 80% of T1 changes inside ±300 ms; at 2.6 s passes the gain mostly disappears, which is why disabling temperature fallback (2.57 to 0.94 s per pass) and capping tokens at 48 (0.53 s) matter so much ([brief §2](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)). The alignment literature says why: Whisper's own timestamp decoder scored a **0.822 s** mean error on sung audio against **0.0709 s** for a CTC head trained on the same encoder, and the authors chose CTC because Whisper's discrete timestamp tokens are "less robust" ([Wang et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.12488)); WhisperX replaced Whisper's word timings with a wav2vec2 phoneme aligner for the same reason and noted that buffered decoding drifts as timestamp errors accumulate ([Bain et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.00747)). Every streaming wrapper adds to that: LocalAgreement-2 averages about **3.3 s** latency on an A40 ([Macháček et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14743)), and Simul-Whisper's authors put latency at one to two chunk lengths with 30 s padding still required ([Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10052)). The two published real-time lyric trackers both run a frame-level phoneme posteriorgram at 40 ms stride with about 280 ms of model delay ([Brazier & Widmer 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.14496)) or a 160 ms buffer at 25 frames per second ([Park et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09200)), and the best online result on classical singing was **75.5% of onsets within 300 ms** against 83.4% offline on the same data ([Park et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09200)). ADR-030's split, words decide which and the onset decides when, is therefore the right architecture; the question is what to add when the onset alone is ambiguous.

The brief's own hypothesis for problem #4, "an onset-triggered first-word spotter" or "a short decode right after an onset for the first-word decision", has two published shapes. The cheaper one is CB-Whisper's design, which runs open-vocabulary keyword spotting on Whisper's encoder hidden states to find user-defined phrases and then feeds them to the decoder ([CB-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09552)); Voxxa already computes the encoder output, and the candidate first lines of the parts reachable from the current position are a tiny keyword set. The more precise one is CTC forced alignment of the second of audio after an onset against those candidate first lines, with the alignment confidence deciding between them. CTC-segmentation does exactly that windowed, confidence-scored matching in O(M) memory and tolerates text that is not in the audio by letting the transcript start anywhere ([Kürzinger et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.09127)); torchaudio's MMS_FA bundle exposes `forced_align()` with a `<star>` token for the same job under a BSD licence ([torchaudio tutorial](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/stable/tutorials/forced_alignment_for_multilingual_data_tutorial.html)); and Huang, Benetos and Ewert's MIT-licensed CRNN phoneme model trained on DALI reached **0.23 s mean / 94% within 0.3 s** on polyphonic Jamendo and expects a vocal-only input, which is what Voxxa's feed is ([Huang et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.01646)) ([LyricsAlignment-MTL](https://github.com/jhuang448/LyricsAlignment-MTL)). The open-source lyric-pilot prototype pairs whisper.cpp with a music-trained CTC model (`mrfakename/w2v-bert-2.0-music-lyrics-ctc`, about 2.33 GB, CPU PyTorch, 1.5 s windows at 0.5 s step) ([lyric-pilot](https://github.com/stephenchenn/lyric-pilot)); that model is far too large for the **814 of 2048 MB** headroom Whisper leaves on the booth GPU, so any aligner Voxxa adopts must be the CRNN-class model or run on CPU, and the brief's budgets (follower ≤500 lines, 1.3× the measured port) mean an aligner is a new module with its own ADR-028 row, not a follower change. No paper benchmarks any of these on a GTX 960, so the claim that a 25 fps CRNN fits beside Whisper Small at 0.53 s per pass is an inference from model size, to be measured, not assumed.

The reference-recording route deserves a lab slot of its own because it attacks two problems the word path cannot. Both online lyric trackers align live audio to a *previous performance* whose lyrics were timed once, not to text ([Brazier & Widmer 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.14496)) ([Park et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09200)); Arzt and Widmer tracked a full symphony with no symbolic score by annotating one recording and aligning six more, and the median of seven parallel OLTW trackers raised orchestral downbeat accuracy within 250 ms from 71–84% to 84–90% ([Arzt & Widmer 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf)). Voxxa's practice recorder already produces exactly that artefact, a capture with tapped slide times, and the open-source Matchmaker library implements OLTW-Arzt with backward-forward correction at **0.07 ms per step** with log-spectral-energy features costing 0.9 ms ([Park et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087)) ([Matchmaker](https://github.com/pymatchmaker/matchmaker)). Run within a part against last rehearsal's capture, such a tracker predicts the next slide time ahead of the words, which is how the ACCompanion's reference-derived tempo expectation reached a 23 ms onset prediction error ([Cancino-Chacón et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12939v2)), and run during a band intro it can arm the first slide the way Brazier's music tracker leads until a voice-dominant part begins ([Brazier & Widmer 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.02592)), which would retire the brief's rule that the first-slide onset needs an operator cue because a cough also fires it. The caveat is structural: OLTW assumes the live performance has the reference's structure, and neither lyric tracker evaluated repeats or skips, so the reference tracker must live *inside* a part under the parts map, never across parts. The perceptual target for the fire itself should be the asymmetric window the one user study found: 193 karaoke participants detected lyrics *ahead* of the audio at **−0.33 s** and *lagging* at **+0.22 s**, so a sub-300 ms early bias is perceptually free while live4's 2.7–5.0 s early moves are far outside any tolerance ([Masclef et al. 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000052.pdf)). A slide change differs from word highlighting, since an early change removes the line the singer is still singing, so the founder's stricter early-fire budget of ≤0.4% per fire (ADR-030) remains defensible; the evidence simply says the budget can be spent on moves up to about 0.3 s early without the congregation noticing.

## Fix the feed before the model: input chain, VAD, prompts and a vocal-only desk output

Three of the four failed live attempts failed on setup, not on following: a start-up pop fired the first slide at 0.6 s, one run had no singing, and one was too quiet with Presenter not in front ([brief §2](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)). The feed numbers explain the "too quiet" class: the booth floor is about −60 dBFS and mostly 60 Hz hum, raw singing sits near −34 dBFS, and through the Silero gate that produced **2 Whisper passes in 200 s** until +12 dB gain gave 152, with live needing about +22 dB and a soft limiter. The literature says this is Silero's mismatch rather than a level bug. Silero's maintainer confirmed in November 2024 that missing songs and very high voices is "a known problem" because the training data contained no music ([silero-vad issue 565](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/issues/565)), and the lyrics-transcription work shows the replacement: an RMS-threshold activity detector on the vocal channel used to set Whisper's segment boundaries cut hallucination rates from **1.31 to 0.35** on one benchmark and **1.09 to 0.12** on another, and lowered long-form WER from 23.02 to 20.35%, with the authors attributing hallucinations to boundaries that cut through vocal activity ([Syed et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15514v1)). Because Voxxa receives an isolated vocal from the console, that gate is near-zero cost: the 100 Hz high-pass, +22 dB gain and tanh limiter already in `live.py`, followed by an energy gate on the 300–4000 Hz band the onset detector already computes, should replace or OR with Silero's 0.60 enter probability, windows should start and end on vocal activity rather than fixed hops, and the level-and-hum check should become a setup health-check item (input-chain ticket, V3b task 6). The product-side rule from the brief's own budget, voxxad practice flags ≤20 lines and the conductor change ≤20 lines, fits this.

The same literature tells Voxxa which recognition errors to design around rather than fix. Whisper's WER on sung recordings of a text averaged **0.56 against 0.14** for the spoken version, with accuracy "less affected by the musical accompaniment and more by the singing style" ([Berendes et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.nlp4musa-1.3/)); it "deletes more than half" of non-lexical vocables and backing vocals even on clean stems ([Syed et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15514v1)); and it emits unrelated text on instrumental sections and varies run to run under temperature sampling ([Zhuo et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17103)). The brief's mishearing of T1's verse-2 first line for about 10 s and the repeated phrases written during held notes are those failure modes, which is why the parts map's rule that dead-reckoning never moves a slide in a labelled song, and its refusal to trust a single window, are correct responses. For the V3c prompt experiment the evidence narrows the design: Whisper's initial prompt is capped at **224 tokens** and attention favours tokens near its end ([arXiv 2410.18363](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18363)), a biasing list cut rare-word WER from 23.7% to 18.0% but a fine-tuned biasing model did far better ([B-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11572)), and the only report of feeding whole lyric text as a prompt says it caused more mistakes ([OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/can-whisper-be-prompted-with-a-previous-transcript/292034)). V3c should therefore test a short spoken-form prompt of the current and next lines at the prompt tail, not the song, and keep the brief's acceptance rule that Base with a prompt wins only if it is no worse on every take, causes no phantom-word move and runs ≤1.0 s per pass. Singer adaptation belongs in the lab queue, not the plan: speech-based in-context learning with two to four same-speaker examples cut dialect-word WER by **32%** for Whisper Small but costs encoder time because example audio shares the 30 s window ([Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.07081v2)), and the only singing LoRA found moved WER from 37.5 to 34.5% ([community LoRA card](https://huggingface.co/Petercoder/autolyrics-whisper-small-lora)).

The band-mix problem (#5) has one credible fix, and it is not software. On P1 the onset detector found only 2 of 11 slide starts because the band fills the breaths, and words ran about 4 s late ([brief §2](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)). Vocal separation at inference does not rescue Whisper: adding Demucs made Jam-ALT WER worse for both large-v2 and large-v3 and caused wrong-language output ([Cífka et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.13987)), real-time separators trade quality for latency (HS-TasNet's authors say causal versions introduce artifacts and "a considerable drop in separation quality" ([Venkatesh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17701)); causal SCNet variants sit at 93 ms ([arXiv 2609.35397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35397))), and none fits beside Whisper in 2 GB of VRAM. A voice-following prompter vendor puts it plainly: "If the app hears the drummer as loudly as the singer, no amount of cleverness fixes that" ([QMe Prompter guide](https://qmeprompter.com/guides/teleprompter-that-follows-your-voice)). FA-39, a vocal-only desk output, is the brief's own hypothesis and the literature agrees it is the cheap fix; until it exists, band songs stay on the plan-order follower in suggest-only mode.

## Self-training is hindsight alignment of rehearsals, never learning on Sunday

The brief's Listener DB (ADR-023, plan V12) is designed but not built: one JSON per song holding learned order, dwell, gaps, outro, words per second and at most 20 heard tokens per slide, proposed by `voxxad --learn` from a rehearsal capture and accepted by `--confirm`, with only confirmed runs feeding the follower ([brief §1](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)). The literature endorses that shape and warns against the alternative. Every follower that learns from rehearsal does so offline between performances: Raphael's Music Plus One learns a soloist's timing "through a series of rehearsals" and retunes a predictive model of note onsets from them ([Raphael 2001](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/2b0f658cbffd284984fb11d90254081f-Paper.pdf)); Nakamura estimated the resume distribution and local transition statistics (0.845 next-chord, 0.113 self-transition) from practice recordings and found piece- and performer-specific habits changed them ([Nakamura et al. 2014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1404.2313)); Arzt and Widmer aligned prior performances offline and used them directly as references, noting that they "implicitly encode a lot of information that is missing in the symbolic score" ([Arzt & Widmer 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000150.pdf)). No published follower adapts its acoustic or pronunciation model online, and no paper adapts any ASR to a single singer from rehearsals, so the brief's hypothesis for problem #7, hindsight alignment of rehearsal captures with the parts map plus per-leader detour statistics, is the state of the art rather than a shortcut.

The concrete recipe has three parts. First, label each capture automatically before anyone taps: forced alignment of the sung-order setlist against the recording with a wav2vec2 CTC aligner (WhisperX's pattern, or MMS_FA) yields word and line timings, with CTC-segmentation's per-segment confidence flagging the lines that did not align ([Bain et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.00747)) ([Kürzinger et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.09127)). Second, route only the uncertain changes to the founder: the active-learning literature recommends auto-accepting confident instances, offering a suggested label for intermediate ones and reserving hand labelling for hard cases, extracting more from each human interaction than plain labelling ([arXiv 2306.01277](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.01277.pdf)); applied to `vocal_test.py`'s tap mode this means the tapper confirms or nudges proposed change marks instead of tapping every slide, and taps are treated as noisy labels, which the existing 524 ms reaction-lag calibration already implies. EvolveCaptions found per-speaker ASR adaptation improved over five rounds with an average of only five minutes of extra recording, and that participants found correction "effortful", acceptable only against expected long-term benefit ([Wu & Jain 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02181)), which is a realistic enrolment budget per song: one or two rehearsal passes plus a short review. Third, derive from each confirmed run the quantities the follower consumes: the order actually sung gives per-leader detour counts, which become Nakamura's resume prior over the handicaps and set `boundary = plan` for leaders who keep the plan (the brief's hypothesis for the 1–3 s post-chorus lag); dwell and gap statistics feed the pace clock; and the capture itself joins the per-song reference pool for the within-part OLTW tracker of step 3. Everything stays on the booth PC, consistent with the brief's privacy rules, and the heard-token cache remains private.

Two HCI findings fix where learning happens. Honeycutt, Nourani and Ragan found that letting users give in-the-loop feedback *lowered* both their trust in a system and their perception of its accuracy ([Honeycutt et al. 2020](https://www.cise.ufl.edu/~eragan/papers/Honeycutt_HCOMP2020.pdf)), and Amershi's guidelines ask systems to "update and adapt cautiously" and "notify users about changes" ([Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)). Live services should therefore be read-only evaluation runs in which the only human inputs are hold, back one and panic, and all learning input comes from rehearsal review, which is what `--confirm` already enforces. The evaluation harness that makes this a loop is mostly built: `voxxad --shadow` is shadow mode in the deployment sense, a candidate that "receives production traffic in parallel ... but does not serve its predictions" ([Statsig](https://statsig.com/perspectives/shadow-deployment-comparison)), and `voxxa-eval` scores it against truth. What is missing is running that comparison automatically after every rehearsal and service and plotting the per-song metrics across rounds; the Music Plus One and EvolveCaptions precedents predict monotone improvement over the first few rounds, and a flat curve on a song is the signal that the band's variability on it needs a different treatment or that the song should stay in suggest-only.

## Volunteers become minders: shared control, a reserved kill key, rehearsal gating and a ten-minute card

The brief's problem #6 asks who corrects a wrong slide when nobody is at the PC, and the human-factors literature answers that the question is posed wrongly. Bainbridge showed that sustained monitoring for rare events is "impossible for even a highly motivated human being", so detecting rare abnormalities "has to be done by an automatic alarm system", while the human needs *more* training for the rare intervention, not less ([Bainbridge 1983](https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/technology/1983-bainbridge.pdf)). Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens separate automation into acquisition, analysis, decision and action stages, each with its own level that may depend on context ([Parasuraman et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354)); for Voxxa, listening and aligning run fully automated always, and the only dial is the action stage, set per song. Amershi's guideline G10, "scope services when in doubt", is the published justification for the brief's amber "not sure" hold instead of a move, and G17, "provide global controls", for a single kill switch ([Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)). Zhang, Liao and Bellamy found that showing confidence calibrated trust but did not by itself improve joint accuracy unless the human could contribute knowledge the system lacked ([Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114)); the worship leader knows they just repeated the chorus, so the useful display is FreeShow's stage view showing the current slide as firm and the predicted next change as provisional, the two-state convention live captioning uses ([USPTO 11741963](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11741963)), with a veto reachable from the stage. The default on uncertainty is the teleprompter rule: PromptSmart's voice tracking "will hold your place, waiting for you to return" when the speaker goes off-script ([PromptSmart Pro](https://apps.apple.com/app/id894811756)), which is the church-tech maxim that "it's better to have nothing on the screen than the wrong thing on the screen" ([Church Visuals](https://churchvisuals.com/article/propresenter-training-guide-for-volunteers)).

Theatre show control supplies the operating discipline. QLab reserves the Escape key exclusively for panic so it stays constant on every system, offers graded stop levels (fade-out panic, stop, hard stop), and its users learned that panic does not reset external gear ([QLab group](https://groups.google.com/g/qlab/c/4dKi891aVns)) ([QLab group](https://groups.google.com/g/qlab/c/qBwlii1XiRU)) ([QLab group](https://groups.google.com/g/qlab/c/fteF6iUa5qg)); Voxxa's equivalent is one unremappable key or Companion button with "hold" (pause following, keep listening) and "panic" (stop all output for the service), a defined post-panic state (current slide held, nothing sent) and a run-book line for what the human does next. Operators keep a single control source and pin exact software versions after the final rehearsal ([QLab group](https://groups.google.com/g/qlab/c/oKHcw1e7BCA)) ([QLab group](https://groups.google.com/g/qlab/c/gtyaChD_cgc)); for Voxxa that means freezing the build, model files and per-song learned state at the last rehearsal, and demoting a song to suggest-only the moment a human presses next manually. The pre-service health check is one green/amber/red screen: vocal signal at expected level with the hum floor measured, FreeShow running and reachable on 5506, today's setlist loaded with each song's rehearsal status, learned-state version matching the last rehearsal, kill-switch test press acknowledged and log file writable; a watchdog on audio frames and alignment updates drops to suggest-only and alarms if either stalls, which is ADR-022's lost and watchdog gates made visible. Rehearsal gating then becomes the promotion rule: a song runs autonomously only if its latest rehearsal or shadow run met the acceptance bar below, otherwise it runs suggest-only, which is shadow-mode promotion applied per item and matches church-slides' and every commercial entrant's assist-and-approve default ([church-slides](https://github.com/martinmain93/church-slides)) ([Kairos](https://kairospresentations.com/)).

Training follows church-tech practice, which is consistent across sources: a one-page role guide, a pre-service checklist, a shutdown guide, practice in a no-pressure setting and a full dry run before each service ([ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/ministry-tech-leaders/2208922-church-tech-audit-checklist.html)); veteran-led rather than director-led training once a volunteer has served about three months ([Church Visuals](https://churchvisuals.com/article/propresenter-training-guide-for-volunteers)); a first-session goal stated as "by the end you can run X" without overwhelming detail ([Life.Church](https://help.life.church/a/852077-video-training-guide)); rotation so nobody serves every week and a short monthly skill session ([ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/ministry-tech-leaders/2210579-tech-volunteers-2.html/2)). A volunteer's role card for "Voxxa minder" therefore has four lines: run the health check and read its colour; know hold, back one and panic; if unsure, hold; who to tell. The ten-minute session is two minutes watching the system follow a rehearsed song in dry run, three minutes practising the override on a deliberately mis-cued replay, three minutes running the checklist alone and two minutes of failure drill, with the end criterion that the trainee can state what the stage colours mean and demonstrate hold and panic. A monthly ten-minute drill in which the founder injects a fault (wrong setlist, muted channel, FreeShow closed) and the volunteer walks the run-book, plus one song a month run manually so the fallback skill survives, answers Bainbridge's de-skilling warning. The context makes the case for the whole programme: confidence with a tech role "usually takes months" of shadowing ([Christianity Today](https://christianitytoday.com/ct/2022/july-web-only/church-volunteer-technology-av-livestream-pandemic-training.html)), and congregations recruit slide operators for one Sunday a month because they cannot staff every week ([First Presbyterian Bellingham](https://fpcbr.org/blog/worship-slide-operators-needed/)); Voxxa's promise is not to remove that person but to shrink the role to a card anyone on the ministry team can hold.

## Acceptance metrics tie the lab's columns to published conventions

Voxxa's `voxxa-eval` already reports right changes, wrong moves, moves more than 1 s early, wrong-slide seconds and the `tight_300` column; the mapping below shows those are the field's standard metrics under other names, and proposes the gates. The thresholds are design proposals for the founder to tune, since no source reports acceptance thresholds for any deployed lyric follower.

| Metric | Definition | Published convention | Voxxa today | Proposed gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Right changes by slide id | Changes whose press lands on the true slide within 1.5 s | Alignment rate at multiple windows, reported per piece and total ([Matchmaker](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087)) | 74 of 77 lab; 11 of 16 live4 ([brief §2](../research_notes/Voxxa%20autonomous%20slide%20following%20research/00_voxxa_current_state_brief.md)) | ≥95% per song in the promotion pass; also report at 0.5 s and 1.0 s |
| Early moves | Signed error e = t_press − t_true below −τ | Signed-error skewness ([Matchmaker](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087)); perceptual thresholds −0.33 s / +0.22 s ([Masclef et al. 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000052.pdf)) | 1 of 77 beyond 1 s lab; 5 of 16 live4 | 0 beyond 0.3 s in the promotion pass; founder decides whether 1.0 s is the live bar |
| tight_300 | Share of changes with abs(e) ≤ 0.3 s | MIREX / mir_eval `percentage_correct` default window 0.3 s ([mir_eval](https://sources.debian.org/src/mir-eval/0.8.2-1/mir_eval/alignment.py)) | 80% T1 with trigger; 70% dry1 | Report; no gate until the aligner lands |
| Wrong-slide seconds | (1 − PCS) × song duration, duration variant that penalises early first and late last changes | `percentage_correct_segments` ([mir_eval](https://sources.debian.org/src/mir-eval/0.8.2-1/mir_eval/alignment.py)) | 112 s over 7 takes; 17.6 s live4 | <10 s per service as a budget |
| Spurious presses and missed changes | Press with no truth change within 2 s; truth change with no press within 2 s | Errors over 2 s excluded from mean error as "unbounded tracking failures" ([Matchmaker](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10087)) | 0 wrong slides dry1 | 0 spurious in the promotion pass and in every live service |
| Lost events | `lost` after 10 s (part) or 20 s (song) | MIREX "piece completion" analogue ([Cont et al. 2007](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2007/paper/000315.pdf)) | New `why` value in ADR-031 | 0 in the promotion pass |
| Latency | First sung word of the new section to press, measured in dry run so I/O counts | Detection-to-report delay ([Cont et al. 2007](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2007/paper/000315.pdf)) | 0.18 s median dry1 | Median ≤0.5 s; verse-after-chorus boundaries ≤1.0 s |
| Early-fire budget | Share of onset fires that move early | ADR-030 | ≤0.4% per fire (design) | Keep; count against the 0.3 s line |
| Overrides and hold time | Human interventions and seconds held per service | Trust-calibration trend ([Akash et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16353v1)) | Not yet logged | Report; trend must fall over successive services |

Service-level acceptance for running without a volunteer is then four consecutive shadow or supervised Sundays with zero early moves beyond the chosen line and zero spurious presses across all songs, wrong-slide seconds under budget, every watchdog check green and two volunteers who have passed the card. A song demotes itself to suggest-only after any live early move or spurious press, and recovers only through a fresh rehearsal pass.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Voxxa's problem. Its recognition and alignment stack is already within sight of the published state of the art for a live lyric follower: no arXiv system tracks sung lyrics online on pop or worship material at all, the two that do it on classical singing reach 75% of onsets within 300 ms, and Voxxa's onset-triggered parts map reaches 80% on its labelled takes. What has been holding autonomy back is the output side, a presentation app that can only be told "next" and "previous" and never asked where it is, and the fix for that is a configuration and migration decision rather than a research programme. Once FreeShow's jump and read-back are proven, the same evidence that validates ADR-031 points to one small addition, treating the breath before a section as the moment when a jump is cheap, and to one architectural commitment, that Whisper is a witness to which part is being sung and never a clock.

The second shift is in what "self-training" and "training the team" mean. Every system that learns from rehearsals does so offline, by aligning the last performance in hindsight and re-estimating priors, and every human-factors result says the live service should accept only hold, back and panic from a person. Voxxa's `--shadow`, `--learn` and `--confirm` flags already encode that split; what remains is to run the comparison automatically, to promote songs one at a time on measured rehearsal evidence, and to hand the ministry team a four-line card rather than a slide deck. The founder's open decisions are mostly about lines in the sand, where to draw early, how many Sundays, which key is panic, and the literature's consistent advice is to draw them conservatively and never move them later.
